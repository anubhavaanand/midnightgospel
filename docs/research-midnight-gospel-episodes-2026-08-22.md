## ⚠️ Critical Corrections to the Request Premise

Before using any of this, note that the episode list in the request is scrambled:

1. **Season 1 has exactly 8 episodes. There is no Episode 9.** [Source #1]
2. **The season/series finale is Episode 8, "Mouse of Silver."**
3. Duncan Trussell's mother in the finale is **Deneen Fendig (1947–2013)** — not "Dortha." She died of stage-four breast cancer roughly three weeks after the podcast interview used in the show was recorded in 2013. [Sources #1, #4, #6]
4. Correct episode map (Wikipedia + fan wiki agree): [Sources #1, #2]

| # | Title | Guest | World |
|---|-------|-------|-------|
| 1 | Taste of the King | Drew Pinsky | Earth 4-169, zombie apocalypse |
| 2 | Officers and Wolves | Anne Lamott, Raghu Markus | Clown World |
| 3 | Hunters Without a Home | Damien Echols | Flooded water world ("Ass Cream") |
| 4 | Blinded By My End | Trudy Goodman | Medieval fantasy world |
| 5 | Annihilation of Joy | Jason Louv | Moon R3T8 Soul Prison |
| 6 | Vulture With Honor | David Nichtern | Buton 78914 (+ home base on the Ribbon) |
| 7 | Turtles of the Eclipse | Caitlin Doughty (as Death) | Blank Ball → tarot world |
| 8 | Mouse of Silver | Deneen Fendig | Simulator interior / cosmic space / train |

Specific corrections: "Mouse of Silver" is Ep8 only (there is no Ep1 "Mouse of Silver"); Drew Pinsky is Ep1; Trudy Goodman is **Ep4** (medieval world), not a meditation-cave episode; Caitlin Doughty plays Death in **Ep7**, not Ep4; Jason Louv/prison is **Ep5** ("Annihilation of Joy"), while "Blinded by My End" (Ep4) is Trudy Goodman's medieval quest.

## Executive Summary

A complete closed-caption-style dialogue transcript of the finale ("Mouse of Silver," S1E8) was recovered from a fan transcription on r/TheMidnightGospel via the Wayback Machine and saved locally as `midnight-gospel-s1e8-mouse-of-silver-transcript.txt`. No full verbatim script exists on official channels; IMDb hosts only key quotes. The underlying interview audio is Duncan Trussell's real DTFH podcast conversation with his dying mother; a near-full auto-transcript of its companion episode ("MY MOM part 2," DTFH #64) exists on Podscripts.co, plus an edited literary excerpt on The Dewdrop. Per-episode setting, character, and visual details below are compiled from Wikipedia, the Midnight Gospel Fandom wiki, TV Tropes, and press coverage, organized for direct translation into game level design.

## Part 2 — Per-Episode World Details for Level Design

### Shared frame (usable across all levels)

- **Home hub:** the Chromatic Ribbon — a membranous, tape-like planet twisting through a colorful void; simulation farmers harvest simulated universes with bio-organic computers. [Source #1]
- **The multiverse simulator** is essentially a vagina-shaped VR helmet; Clancy projects into avatars. Exiting = blowing a shofar → third eye opens on forehead → skyward exit. [Source #4]
- **Rule of thumb per level:** every visited world is apocalyptic/dying; planets carry X marks ("Because they are dying"); when Clancy exits, the planet explodes behind him. [Sources #1, #4]
- Global aesthetic: bright unnatural creature colors, ultra-violent slapstick carnage under calm philosophical talk; cited influences include Adventure Time, Watership Down, The Last Unicorn, Æon Flux, Twin Peaks. [Source #1]

### Ep1 — "Taste of the King"
- **Setting:** Earth 4-169 mid-zombie-apocalypse; failed sim Earth 1-9432 shown first (humans dead from "operator error"). White House exterior with anti-marijuana protesters ignoring shambling zombies; secret bookshelf-door library escape; pool-table room. [Sources #1, #2]
- **Characters:** Glasses Man (diminutive gun-toting President; HUD stats display Devanagari-script gunas + "truthfulness"), Peggy (pregnant woman helped through birth before zombification), dog Charlotte (fur contains a parallel universe/dimensional stomach — series regular afterward), drone named Munnin, giant zombie Chef Dardew Pepper. Clancy avatar: tanned beach body. [Sources #1, #2, #4]
- **Visual keys:** suburban Americana overrun by undead while protesters wave signs; weapons/billiard balls stashed in dog fur. Ends: a cure revives everyone so zombies can re-kill them; Clancy escapes with Charlotte as Earth explodes. [Sources #1, #4]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** sunny daylight comedy-horror; gore played for laughs against warm suburbia.

### Ep2 — "Officers and Wolves"
- **Setting:** "Clown World," entered after Clancy's computer catches a pop-up-ad virus. Deer-dogs devour baby clowns; industrial slaughterhouse converts creatures into sentient meat-mush; robot clown-head parasites (spider-limbed, puppeteering bodies) control the populace; resistance uprising destroys the plant. Trio devoured by giant fly, regurgitated to maggots. [Sources #1, #4]
- **Characters:** Anne Deer Dog (Anne Lamott), Raghu Deer Dog (Raghu Markus), clown babies.
- **Clancy avatar:** virus-corrupted Abraxas-style chimera — chicken head, morning-star right arm, tentacle left arm, snakes for feet. [Source #4]
- **Visual keys:** clown heads styled like Irish "Billy Roll" meat-brand cylinders ("meat clowns"); souvenir green glass slippers; Clown World ends exploding into a swarm of giant flies. [Sources #1, #4]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** carnival colors drenched in industrial pink/red meat-processing horror.

### Ep3 — "Hunters Without a Home"
- **Setting:** completely flooded ocean world (labeled "Ass Cream"); ice-covered region needing a special traversal device; elaborate ship with non-Euclidean geometry; set-piece inside the ear/head of sleeping giant Barry, rock creatures queueing to enter his brain. [Sources #1, #4]
- **Characters:** Darryl the fish(-cyborg) man with an all-cat crew; Barry the sleeping giant; subscriber Daniel Hoops and abusive father Magistrate Hoops (recurring antagonist).
- **Visual keys:** ice cream-puking pegasus souvenir; giant-vs-giant brawl cracks the planet's crust and detonates the world. [Sources #1, #4]
- **Dialogue themes:** prison, magic (Crowley/Thelema), meditation-as-magic. [Source #1]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** deep-sea blues and glacial whites; nautical cosmic horror wrapped around serene spiritual talk.

### Ep4 — "Blinded By My End"
- **Setting:** intended party planet "Mercuritaville" missed after a ship full of cats knocks Clancy off course; instead a medieval-fantasy kingdom. Witch's hut; duel arena; Prince Jam Roll revealed as monstrous demon whose death melts the entire planet. [Sources #1, #4]
- **Characters:** Trudy the Love Barbarian (Trudy Goodman) with healing rose; boyfriend Gerald (killed, resurrected); villain Prince Jam Roll (Pauly Shore).
- **Souvenir:** the healing rose (recurring object; vomited on in Ep5, worshipped by rats). [Sources #1, #4]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** dark storybook fantasy — saturated heraldic colors curdling into apocalypse.

### Ep5 — "Annihilation of Joy"
- **Setting:** hidden world Moon R3T8 — a Soul Prison for wayward souls who bit off their own tongues under existential dread; holds souls of characters from earlier episodes; crash rubble; escape loops; stalagmite impalement. "You cannot die in a soul prison. You can only be reborn." [Sources #1, #2]
- **Characters:** prisoner Bob (speaking voice Eddie Pepitone, singing voice Johanna Warren); soul bird Jason (Jason Louv; psychopomp/whip-poor-will) attached by a Soul String; rainbow avatar Nell Noheem.
- **Mechanics/iconography:** the "Bardo Loop" — each death triggers an Egyptian weighing-of-the-heart vs a feather; guilty hearts relive the escape; Bob finally wins a clock-heart that alters time, regrows his tongue, sings. Dialogue covers Bardo Thödol, Indra's Net, hope/hopelessness. [Sources #1, #2, #4]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** purgatorial dungeon gloom punctured by psychedelic Egyptian-afterlife golds.

### Ep6 — "Vulture With Honor"
- **Setting A (home base):** the Ribbon neighborhood — sister Sarah's call; neighbors Blithfreyus/Shanefreyus/Stephreyus (artifact-thieving sim farmers); overheating steaming simulator; lantern-creature oil fetch; Captain Bryce lost in a "wobble"; malfunctioning sim glitch-speech ("you can never see your own eyes") spawning pie-based lifeforms. [Sources #1, #2, #4]
- **Setting B:** planet Buton 78914 — Clancy visits as an **Octopus Sheriff** to see friend David (David Nichtern) for a meditation lesson (seeing what's around you vs clearing the mind). [Source #1]
- **Maintenance lore:** green oil prevents catastrophic simulator "Wobble" events (foreshadows finale). [Sources #1, #4]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** cluttered domestic sci-fi glitchiness resolving into calm desert-western sheriff world.

### Ep7 — "Turtles of the Eclipse"
- **Setting:** Blank Ball — featureless white sphere where Clancy clones himself in cream; boredom spawns a water slide; digging for a hose drops him into a tarot-themed dreamworld. Chase of "The Fool" creature; Death executes him via ping-pong balls (predicted death); cliff fall; prophecy that Clancy dies in a rolly chair. [Sources #1, #4]
- **Characters:** Death — classic grim reaper silhouette with a "fun eyeball" (Caitlin Doughty). [Source #1]
- **Themes:** American institutionalized death care, the "Death Industrial Complex" (Civil War embalming marketing). [Source #4]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** minimalist white void → tarot-card surrealism; memento-mori comedy.

### Ep8 — "Mouse of Silver" (finale)
- **Setting progression (a ready-made three-act level arc):**
  1. **Simulator interior:** mom arrives aboard a spaceship crewed by teddy-bear scientists studying love/happiness; Clancy regresses to childhood; both age rapidly; after her death Clancy becomes pregnant and births an infant Deneen; stage-four cancer reveal; "you cry" beat.
  2. **Cosmic ascent:** they rise as spirits, then become planets discussing why people avoid death; Deneen is pulled into a black hole. [Sources #1, #2, #4]
  3. **Ribbon catastrophe & coda:** Magistrate Hoops' police raid the trailer; Charlotte takes a bullet for Clancy; both get sucked into the simulator as it explodes, releasing purple "wobbles"; Blithfreyus dives into his own sim with his kids; Clancy awakens in an Escher-esque void and boards an infinitely long Afterlife Express bus/train carrying every dead character from the season; Ram Dass: "Just be here now."; final voicemail; Joe Wong's song "Dreams Wash Away" closes. [Sources #1, #2, #4]
- **Unique mechanics noted by wiki/tropes:** only episode without Clancy's meteor-drop launch; over-staying in the sim makes it steam up and blur real/virtual; teddy bears recur throughout. [Sources #2, #4]
- **Mood/palette (interpretive):** pastel cosmic nursery tones and warm teddy-bear softness → domestic chaos → transcendent starfield; the emotional inversion of every prior episode.

- **Source episode attribution:** Wikipedia credits DTFH Episode 42 ("MY MOM", Oct 2012) for S1E8; The Dewdrop and remaster uploads tie the deathbed material to "MY MOM part 2"/related sessions (March 2013, weeks before her death). Both may feed different beats of the finale. Unresolved.
- **Train vs bus:** sources variously call the finale vehicle a train or a bus (TV Tropes uses both terms in different entries).
- User-supplied premise (9 episodes, "Dortha," several guest/world pairings) conflicts with every authoritative source; corrected above.
